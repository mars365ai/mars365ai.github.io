# PRD Decisions

This document records the discovery questions asked while shaping the Mars365 AI public website, the recommendation for each, and the answer accepted by the user. An accepted recommendation is the product decision unless later changed.

## Product Decisions

### Q1. Public identity

**Question:** Should the site use “Mars365 AI” as a provisional, configurable public brand, while leaving unconfirmed company details and claims out?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Use Mars365 AI as the provisional identity; make company details configurable and do not invent history, credentials, customer results, or contact details.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q2. Primary audience

**Question:** Should the site prioritize CIOs, CTOs, and digital-transformation leaders evaluating AI for business operations, with the other listed audiences secondary?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Lead with the concerns and decisions of those buyers, while keeping the content clear to non-technical visitors.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q3. Technology positioning

**Question:** Should Mars365 AI be presented as vendor-neutral, with Microsoft AI as one prominent service area rather than the company's central specialization?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Describe the intended range of capabilities without implying unverified partnerships or certifications.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q4. Meaning of “solutions”

**Question:** Should the solution library represent illustrative use cases and possible approaches, rather than products or proven customer deployments?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Label examples as illustrative and describe benefits as possibilities, not guarantees.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q5. Contact conversion

**Question:** Should “Let's Talk AI” point to a configurable business email, with no form and no fabricated fallback contact details?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. When an address is configured, provide a functional `mailto:` link and copy-email action. Do not implement form submission or invent an address.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q6. Launch scope

**Question:** Should the first release include every page type listed in the PRD, using representative local content even where real company material is not yet available?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Build the full listed site structure, with unknown details configurable and unverified examples clearly marked as illustrative.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q7. Service claims

**Question:** Should the six service areas in the PRD be treated as Mars365 AI's intended offerings, without implying a delivery track record?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Present them as services, but make no claims about prior clients, deployments, or results.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q8. Technology claims

**Question:** Should technologies appear on the site only when explicitly enabled in company configuration, with none assumed by default?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Treat the technology list as an allowlist of confirmed capabilities, not a list inferred from the PRD.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q9. Demonstration content

**Question:** Should launch content include the six named illustrative solutions, all eight named industry pages, and three locally authored Insights articles?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Clearly label illustrative examples and avoid invented company-specific evidence.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q10. Language

**Question:** Should launch content be English-only, while keeping the site structure amenable to adding languages later?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Do not add translation workflows or other languages in the first release.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q11. Analytics and tracking

**Question:** Should the launch site omit analytics, tracking pixels, and marketing cookies, using browser storage only for the requested theme preference?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Do not add visitor analytics unless explicitly approved and reflected in the privacy policy.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

### Q12. Reference use

**Question:** Should we borrow the reference site's promise → customer problems → benefits → credibility → FAQ → CTA sequence, but not its branding, copy, imagery, or specific claims?

**Recommended answer:** Yes. Use the section flow as inspiration and create an original Mars365 AI experience.

**Accepted answer:** Yes.

**Reference:** [Involv Intranet](https://www.involv-intranet.com/). Its home page leads with a concise promise, then presents customer problems, benefits, credibility, FAQs, and a final CTA. These are structural observations only; its company-specific claims, wording, branding, and imagery are not to be reused.

## Agreed Content Boundaries

- The six service categories are intended offerings, but not evidence of past delivery.
- Technology capabilities remain hidden until explicitly confirmed in company configuration.
- Solutions and sample Insights are demonstration content, not products, case studies, or customer results.
- Benefits are potential outcomes, not guarantees.
- Unknown company facts and contact details remain absent until supplied.
- The launch site is English-only and has no analytics or marketing tracking.
